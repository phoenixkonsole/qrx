Unique selling point

QRX is built as a quantum-ready, lightweight chain with local-first wallet control.

Your keys stay local. The wallet can run as a safe home wallet, a passive delegator, or an advanced validator node. Core consensus stays clean, transparent and independent.

Operating modes

Choose the right mode: Wallet, Delegator, or Validator.

Wallet Mode

Send, receive and keep a local wallet. You may run the daemon without producing blocks. No slashing risk.

Delegator Mode

Delegate an exact amount of QUB to an existing validator. You keep your wallet and private keys. Your computer may be offline after the transaction confirms. Delegation increases that validator’s consensus power; it does not make your wallet a validator and it does not transfer ownership of your wallet.

Validator Mode

Run a block-producing node with self-stake. This requires stable 24/7 uptime and carries slashing risk.

Best practice

Wallet/Delegator Mode may go offline. Validator Fleet hosts must stay online while they are validating.

Recommended setup:

Use Wallet Mode by default and Delegator Mode for passive participation. Validator Mode can run on your M1 or a server, but the host must stay awake, connected and synchronized. A fleet shares one node runtime; do not launch one daemon per validator wallet.

Staking

Delegator Mode is safe for home users.You can delegate QUB and close the wallet. Slashing/offline penalties apply to validators, not to normal wallet users. Self-staking makes this wallet a validator candidate and requires Validator Mode.
Validator risk:

Only self-stake if Validator Mode is enabled and this machine/server is intended to run reliably. Minimum self-stake is 100 QUB.

Delegation in one sentence:your QUB stays attributed to your wallet but its staking power is assigned to a validator. You donotneed to keep this Mac online and Delegator Mode does not turn your wallet into a validator.
1 · Choose validatorPaste the validator's qrx1… address. Only validators with enough self-stake can accept delegation.
2 · Choose QUBOnly the amount you enter is delegated. The wallet never delegates your whole balance automatically.
3 · You may go offlineThe validator operates the node. Rewards depend on validator performance and commission.
4 · Leave laterUndelegate starts the protocol unbonding period. Claim becomes possible after that period matures.
Tip: copy this from Validator Set / the validator operator. Delegation never gives the validator your private key.
Example: entering 25 delegates exactly 25 QUB — not the rest of the wallet.

Stop delegation / get QUB back

Step 1 starts undelegation. Step 2 claims the matured amount after the unbonding period. This is intentionally two-step so stake cannot disappear from consensus instantly.

Slashing:delegators do not run a node and are not penalized for their own computer being offline. The selected validator can still be slashed according to consensus rules, which can affect delegated stake/rewards depending on the protocol rule.

Manage many validator identities through one QRX node. Selecting or enabling an address NEVER moves QUB. A normal address becomes eligible to validate only after it has the required self-stake collateral. Bootstrap addresses already have their 1,000 QUB Genesis self-stake, so enabling them can make them validator-ready immediately if the key is unlocked and the node is online.
